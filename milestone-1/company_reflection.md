# Understanding Focus Bear's Mission & Vision  

**Milestone:** 1  
**Issue Number:** #53  
**Date:** 23/10/2026  

## What I Found on the Focus Bear Website

- Focus Bear calls itself **"a gentle focus coach for busy brains"**.
- It is **"Built by AuDHDers for AuDHDers"**, which means it was made by and for people with ADHD and autism.
- It helps people have distraction-free work sessions, build healthy habits and routines, and make deep focus a normal part of their day.

![Focus Bear website](screenshots/focus-bear-website.png)

## Reflection

### Why do you think Focus Bear was created?

- To help people **focus on their work without getting distracted**.
- It is mainly made for **neurodivergent people**, such as people with ADHD, autism or dyslexia, who can find it harder to stay focused.
- But anyone who struggles to focus can use it, **including me**.

### What problem is Focus Bear solving?

- The problem of **distractions**, especially the "just one short break" that turns into hours.
- It helps people **calmly focus** on one thing at a time.
- It also helps people **build routines**, so the day has a clear structure and they don't have to keep deciding what to do next.

### Why do you think this mission is important?

- Losing focus doesn't just waste time. **It also makes people feel bad afterwards.** I know this feeling myself (see below).
- For some neurodivergent people, things like **loud noises and bright colours can be overwhelming**, which makes focusing even harder. A calm, distraction-free space helps them do their work properly.
- Reminders to **take proper breaks** and **simple daily routines** help people keep going without burning out.

### How does Focus Bear's work align with my personal values or interests?

- I care about using my time well, especially during exams, and Focus Bear helps me do that.
- I like that it doesn't just block things. It also makes sure I **rest properly**, which I think is just as important.

### Do I personally relate to any of the challenges that Focus Bear aims to solve?

**Yes, a lot, especially with distractions while studying.**

#### What distracts me most

- **YouTube** (mostly **Shorts**) and **online games**, like Monopoly on the **richup** website and other small game and puzzle sites.
- Sometimes I watch **series** or read **novels** that have nothing to do with my studies.
- I don't use Instagram, and I rarely use my phone. I do almost everything on my **laptop**, so my laptop is also where my biggest distractions are.

#### When it happens

- Mostly **before exams**.
- After I finish studying one topic in detail, I feel I need a break, so I open YouTube Shorts or a game "for a few minutes", **and then I don't come back**.

#### A real example

- When studying for an exam, I open the slides for a topic, get through maybe **10–15 slides**, and then lose interest.
- I start watching Shorts or a series, or reading a novel.
- Usually I lose **30 minutes to 1 hour** before I force myself to start again.
- Sometimes it goes up to **3–4 hours**, and then I **regret** wasting that time.

#### How Focus Bear helps me

- **Micro-breaks:**
  - During a micro-break, Focus Bear **locks my laptop**, so I can't open YouTube or games.
  - So I take a **real break**: I go out to my **balcony**, do a few **exercises**, have some **snacks**, and come back **refreshed and full of energy**, instead of getting stuck on Shorts for hours.
- **Blocking super distracting sites:**
  - I blocked sites like **YouTube**, so I can't open them while I'm doing important work.
  - The **strictness levels** let me change how strict the blocking is depending on what kind of work I'm doing.
- **Using both together:** during breaks my laptop is locked anyway, and during work the blocker stops me opening distracting sites. Together, that closes the "one quick break" trap that usually costs me hours.
- **Brain Dump:**
  - I write down what I've finished, and **what I'll do next**.
  - This works like a small goal: finish this part in a set time, then move to the next topic.

#### How routines help me

- On **weekdays**, when I'm working on something or have exams, I follow a routine. It helps me **break my day into parts** and balance work and rest.
- **My exam routine:**
  - Wake up around **4 am** (I focus best early in the morning) and study until about **8:30 am**.
  - Breakfast and a bath, then study again from about **10:30 am to 2 pm**.
  - Lunch, then study again from about **3:30 pm to 7:30 pm**, when I finish for the day because I'm tired.
- **Micro-breaks** between these study blocks really help me keep this routine going through the day without getting exhausted.

#### How Focus Bear could help someone at uni

- I don't personally know anyone with ADHD or autism. I've heard people talk about it and watched a few documentaries about how hard it can be for them to focus.
- But I am a **uni student** myself, so I know how it feels. I'm good with **deadlines**, but I struggle with **distractions while studying for exams**.
- For a student like me, or a classmate who struggles more than I do, Focus Bear could help by:
  - **blocking** the sites that steal hours (YouTube, games)
  - forcing **real breaks** away from the screen, instead of "breaks" that turn into hours on Shorts
  - helping build a **simple daily study routine**, so the day doesn't fall apart
  - using **Brain Dump** to set small goals for each study block
- For someone with ADHD or autism, I think this structure and calm, distraction-free space would matter **even more** than it does for me.
