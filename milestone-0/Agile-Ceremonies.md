## Main Agile Ceremonies and Their Purpose

- **Daily Stand-ups**
  - A short, regular check-in, usually around 15 minutes.
  - Each person shares what they've worked on, what they're doing next, and anything blocking them.
  - **Purpose:** keep everyone aligned and spot blockers early. It's not for solving problems in detail; those go to a separate chat afterwards.
- **Sprint Planning (Scrum) vs Continuous Prioritization (Kanban)**
  - **Sprint Planning (Scrum):**
    - At the start of each sprint, the team picks the work it commits to for the next 1–4 weeks.
    - Scope is then mostly fixed until the sprint ends.
  - **Continuous Prioritization (Kanban):**
    - No sprints. The backlog is kept in priority order all the time.
    - When someone finishes a task, they pull the next most important one.
    - Priorities can change any day.
  - **Purpose of both:** make sure the team works on the most valuable things first.
- **Retrospectives**
  - A regular meeting to look back on how the team worked, not just what it built.
  - Usually covers what went well, what didn't, and what to try next time, ending with a few **action items**.
  - **Purpose:** continuous improvement of the process and teamwork.
- **Backlog Refinement**
  - Reviewing upcoming backlog items before they're worked on:
    - clarifying requirements
    - adding acceptance criteria
    - splitting big tasks into smaller ones
    - estimating or re-ordering
  - **Purpose:** when someone pulls the next task, it's clear and ready to start.

## How Agile Teams Collaborate Asynchronously and Across Time Zones

- **Written updates instead of meetings:** async stand-ups in Slack or a channel, where each person posts their update when their day starts.
- **A shared board** (e.g. GitHub Projects) is the single source of truth for who's doing what, so nobody has to ask.
- **Issues and pull requests carry the context:** clear descriptions, screenshots, and comments on what changed.
- **Recorded meetings or written meeting notes** for people who couldn't attend live.
- **Overlap hours:** keep the few hours when time zones overlap for live discussions that really need them.
- **Clear handoffs:** before logging off, leave a note on what's done, what's next and what's blocked, so someone in another time zone can continue.
- **Agreed response times**, so people know when to expect an answer and don't sit blocked.

## Reflection

### How do Agile ceremonies help with communication and alignment?

- **Stand-ups** make everyone's work visible, so people know what others are doing and can spot overlaps or blockers early.
- **Planning and prioritization** make sure everyone agrees on what matters most right now.
- **Backlog refinement** makes requirements clear **before** work starts, which reduces misunderstandings and rework.
- **Retrospectives** give a safe, regular place to raise problems with how the team works, so they get fixed instead of building up.
- In a **remote team**, these regular touchpoints replace the casual conversations an office would give you.

### Which ceremony is most important for my role, and why?

- **The daily stand-up**, or its async written version.
- As a remote backend intern, I can easily get stuck on something (e.g. an error, a setup problem, or not understanding a task) and lose hours without anyone knowing.
- Stand-ups make it normal to share **blockers** early and get help quickly.
- They also keep me aware of what others are working on, which matters when my backend work connects to theirs.

## Task

### One change I can make to improve team collaboration

- **Share blockers early and in writing, with context.**
- My retrospective showed how useful it is to clearly list what's done and what's left. I'll apply the same idea to my daily work:
  - When I'm stuck for more than about 30–45 minutes, I'll post a short message with:
    - what I'm trying to do
    - the exact error
    - what I already tried
  - I'll keep my GitHub issues and Kanban board up to date, so my status is visible without anyone having to ask.
- This way, the team can help me sooner, and my progress is clear in stand-ups.
